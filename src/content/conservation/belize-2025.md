In May–June 2025, Jacob joined [Clearwater Marine Aquarium Research Institute](https://mission.cmaquarium.org/research-institute/manatee-research/belize-manatee-conservation/) for manatee health assessments in Belize. The work is research, not a swim-with-wildlife trip. Teams locate animals in shallow water, bring them aboard for a timed exam, and release them.

[Jamal Galves](https://www.neaq.org/person/jamal-galves/) leads the Belize program. He invited Jacob after they met at CMA in 2024. Field mentors that season included [Dr. Adrienne Adkins](https://www.cmaquarium.org/), [Glenford Guy](https://www.cmaquarium.org/), Dr. Bob Bonde, and other named scientists on the boats.

The first week was based at **Gales Point Manatee**, a coastal village that CMA already describes as part of this program. The second stretch was out of **Placencia**.

On the water, Jacob learned the exam sequence used on wild West Indian manatees: keep the animal wet and calm, watch respiration, and help with sampling and records.

**Day 1** included a large male the team called **Rocko**. Jacob listened to the heartbeat and watched the physical exam. He also learned how blood is drawn and processed for the lab.

**Day 2** had two captures. The first animal was taken during mating; the team recorded reproductive material as part of that exam. The second was a male they named **Jake**. Jacob helped collect a urine sample, assisted with blood sampling, repositioned a pectoral flipper, and kept the animal wet and distracted while others took a tissue sample from the paddle (the tail) and placed a tag under the skin.

Later days included a boat-strike survivor whose wounds the veterinarian cleaned, two females in Placencia, and an adult male of more than 800 lb fitted with a **belt tag** — a temporary, reusable tag the team described as designed to release on its own after months of tracking. One weather day went to a long drone search instead of captures. Jacob drove a support boat that afternoon.

**Last water day:** the team released one animal because the water was too deep for a safe capture. The second was **Todd**, more than **860 lb**. Jacob helped stimulate breaths by wetting the snout when respiration paused for more than about a minute, then helped with the release.

After the expedition he spoke to the **Rotary Club of Etowah** as an aspiring marine biologist.

These weights, names, and procedures are from the 2025 trip. They are not population statistics. CMA’s [Belize manatee page](https://mission.cmaquarium.org/research-institute/manatee-research/belize-manatee-conservation/) and [NOAA](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/west-indian-manatee) publish the public science and rules.
